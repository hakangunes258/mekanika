### Full Script:

**[0:00-0:20] Introduction**

"Welcome to Mekanika's Interference Fit Calculator tutorial.

Whether you're designing a new shaft-hub connection or verifying an existing design, this calculator will help you determine contact pressure, assembly forces, and safety factors in seconds."

**[0:20-1:00] Theory Basics**

"An interference fit creates a force-locked connection between a shaft and hub through elastic deformation. The shaft diameter is intentionally larger than the hub bore, creating interference.

DIN 7190 is the German standard that provides calculation methods for cylindrical interference joints.

The key parameters we'll calculate are:
- Contact pressure between shaft and hub
- Required assembly or disassembly force
- Safety factors against yielding and slipping
- Temperature effects for shrink fitting"

**[1:00-5:00] Calculator Demo**

"Let's design a shrink fit connection for a 50 millimeter diameter shaft.

First, navigate to mekanika.org and select 'Interference Fit Calculator'.

Step 1: Shaft Dimensions
- Shaft diameter: 50 millimeters
- Shaft length: 80 millimeters

Step 2: Hub Dimensions
- Hub inner diameter: Same as shaft - we'll specify interference separately
- Hub outer diameter: 100 millimeters
- Hub length: 80 millimeters

Step 3: Interference
- Minimum interference: 0.04 millimeters
- Maximum interference: 0.06 millimeters
- We'll use 0.05 millimeters for this example

Step 4: Materials
- Shaft material: C45 steel (also known as AISI 1045)
- Hub material: GG-25 cast iron

Step 5: Loading
- Applied torque: 500 Newton-meters
- Safety factor requirement: 1.5 (standard for normal operation)

Now click Calculate.

The results show:
- Contact pressure: 45.2 megapascals
- Assembly force: 125 kilonewtons - this means we'll need a hydraulic press
- Safety factor against yielding: 2.1 - this exceeds our requirement of 1.5
- Safety factor against slipping: 1.8 - also safe
- Temperature difference for shrink fitting: 180 degrees Celsius"

**[5:00-6:00] Results Interpretation**

"Let's understand these results:

Contact pressure of 45.2 MPa is within safe limits for our materials. It's well below the yield strength of both shaft and hub.

The safety factor of 2.1 means our joint can handle twice the design load before yielding occurs. DIN 7190 recommends a minimum safety factor of 1.5 for normal operation, so we're good.

Assembly force of 125 kilonewtons means manual press-fitting is not possible - we need hydraulic equipment. Alternatively, we can use shrink fitting by heating the hub.

The calculator shows we need to heat the hub by 180 degrees Celsius for easy assembly. This is practical for cast iron hubs.

Always verify both shaft and hub stresses are within material limits - the calculator does this automatically and highlights any concerns in red."

**[6:00-6:30] Tips & Best Practices**

"Here are key tips for interference fit design:

One: Consider temperature differences during operation. Thermal expansion can significantly affect contact pressure.

Two: Use proper lubrication for press fitting to reduce assembly forces by 20-30 percent.

Three: For shrink fitting, ensure uniform heating to avoid thermal stress.

Four: Always check both shaft and hub stresses - the weakest component determines your design.

Five: Export your results as PDF for documentation and quality records.

The calculator includes all these considerations in its analysis."

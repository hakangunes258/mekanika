How an internal gear pair is calculated

An internal gear pair is a pinion running inside a ring gear (annulus). The two turn the same way, the centre distance is the difference of the pitch radii, and the concave flank of the annulus wraps around the convex flank of the pinion. That conformity is the whole attraction: the contact stress is lower, the path of contact longer and the sliding smaller than on an external pair with the same teeth, in a package where the shafts sit close together.

The rating is the same ISO 6336 chain as for an external pair — root bending, flank pitting, scuffing, micropitting — with the internal branches of each standard. What is genuinely new are the interferences: an annulus can foul the pinion in ways an external wheel cannot, and those are geometric show-stoppers that no safety factor reveals. The calculator checks them before anything else.

Where the inputs come from

Profile shift. On an annulus a positive x2 moves the profile towards the tooth tip — towards the axis — which thickens the tooth but makes the tip diameter smaller, pushing it towards the pinion's fillet. Most internal pairs need a clearly negative x2 to keep the annulus tip on the pinion's involute, which is what the default pair below does. The price is a tighter annulus root fillet: watch the notch parameter qs of the annulus, which ISO 6336-3 only covers up to 8.

Tooth number difference. z2 − z1 is limited by tip interference. For unshifted 20° full-depth teeth on a 40-tooth pinion it has to be at least 9; a larger pressure angle, profile shift or shorter tips let it come down.

Rim outside diameter. A ring gear is often thin. ISO 6336-3 covers it only down to a rim of 1,75·mn under the root; below that the rim cracks radially before a tooth breaks, and the calculator says so.

Reference profile. Annuli are shaped with a pinion-type cutter, not hobbed. Enter the cutter's addendum and tip radius as the reference profile; the root fillet radius in particular drives the stress correction factor of the annulus. The default uses ISO 53 profile A (ρ*fP = 0,38): with the tighter profile B (0,30) the annulus fillet of the same pair falls outside the range the stress correction formula is valid for.

Look at the flank. The nominal contact stress of 341,6 MPa carries √((u−1)/u) where an external pair carries √((u+1)/u) — at u = 2,5 that alone is a factor of 0,65 — and the longer path of contact lowers Zε as well. The concave annulus is doing exactly what it is there for.

The root tells the opposite story for the annulus. Its form factor is low (0,872: the tooth widens towards the root), but its fillet is tight, so the stress correction factor is high (3,603) and the annulus, not the pinion, sets the minimum root safety at 3,16.

Now the reason for the shifts. Unshifted, at the reference centre distance of 27 mm, the annulus tip engages the pinion at dNf1 = 33,885 mm — below the pinion's root form circle of 34,014 mm, i.e. on its fillet, not its involute. Opening the centre distance to 27,5 mm (x2 = −0,355) still leaves it 20 µm short. Only around x2 = −0,69 does the checks card go green, and every one of those designs is rated anyway — a safety factor says nothing about whether the gears can run.

Reading the result

The interference checks come first. A red row there means the gears cannot be assembled or run as drawn, whatever the safety factors say. The usual cures are a positive x1, a negative x2 (a larger annulus tip diameter), more teeth on the annulus, or a larger pressure angle.

Control dimensions. Neither a span micrometer nor a tooth caliper reaches an annulus flank, so the annulus is measured between balls. A thinner tooth lets the balls move outwards, so on the annulus both permitted limits sit above the theoretical Mi — the opposite of the pinion.

Backlash and the housing. On an internal pair the pinion moves towards the annulus teeth as the centre distance grows, so a looser housing closes the mesh. The minimum backlash therefore occurs at the largest centre distance.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use this for a planetary gearbox?

For one planet–ring mesh in isolation, yes, with the planet as the pinion. The load sharing between planets, the mesh with the sun and the planet bearing loads are not modelled.

Why is there no 3D model on this page?

The viewer draws external pairs. Showing that next to an internal calculation would draw a gear the calculation does not describe, so the internal page leaves it out until it has a model of its own.

Why does the annulus have no undercut limit?

Undercut is a rack-generation effect on an external gear. An annulus has its own limits instead — the tip lying outside its base circle and the interference conditions — and those are in the checks card.

Which standard is this?

ISO 21771 geometry, ISO 6336 parts 1, 2, 3 and 5 with their internal-gear branches, ISO 1328-1:2013 tolerances, ISO/TR 10064-2 backlash, ISO/TR 13989 scuffing and ISO/TR 15144-1 micropitting. The tip interference condition is the classical one, checked here against a direct simulation of the mesh.
